# Communication Tone Profile — United States (Austin office)

*Common workplace tendency, not a rule for every individual — see DOC-COMM-001.*

- **Directness:** Often fairly direct, frequently paired with positive framing
  ("Great progress — one thing to adjust before Friday...").
- **Formality:** Generally informal; first names and casual tone in chat are standard
  from day one, including with senior leadership.
- **Disagreement:** Often raised fairly openly, sometimes framed constructively
  ("I see it a bit differently — here's why") rather than left unsaid.
- **Feedback:** Frequently structured with a positive opener before the critical point
  ("sandwich" style is a common, though not universal, tendency).
- **Urgency:** Often communicated explicitly and sometimes with informal intensifiers
  ("this is pretty time-sensitive") alongside a stated deadline.
- **Meetings:** Tend to start close to on time; a degree of small talk before getting to
  business is common but usually brief.

## What May Unintentionally Land Wrong
- **Too indirect:** Excessive hedging can read as lacking confidence in the request.
- **Too direct:** Criticism with no positive framing at all can land as unnecessarily
  harsh, even when the content is fair.
- **Appropriate example:** "Nice work on the draft — can we tighten section 2 and get a
  revised version by Thursday?"

## Individual Preference Reminder
Austin is a relatively small Nexora office (Sales and Customer Success); individual
communication style varies by role and tenure as much as by geography.
