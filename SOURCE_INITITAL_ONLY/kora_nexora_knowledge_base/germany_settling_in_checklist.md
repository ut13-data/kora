# Germany Settling-In Checklist

General guidance and reminders for employees relocating to Munich or Berlin. Not legal
or government advice — check current official guidance for your location and
circumstances wherever noted.

## Housing
- Finding and registering a home is usually the first practical step, since many later
  steps (bank account, address registration) require a fixed address.
- Nexora's Office Manager (Munich) can share a list of relocation-friendly agencies used
  by past new joiners, but does not manage housing directly.

## Address Registration (Anmeldung)
- Registering your address with the local registration office is a required step for
  most residents in Germany and is generally expected within a short window after
  moving in.
- **Check the current official guidance for your location and circumstances** — exact
  deadlines and required documents can vary and change; do not rely solely on this
  checklist for the legal requirement itself.

## Bank Account
- Most new joiners open a German bank account within the first couple of weeks, as
  payroll is paid in EUR to a German (or SEPA-reachable) account.
- Several major banks and app-based "neobanks" are commonly used by internationals;
  HR can share what past joiners have used, informally, on request.

## Health Insurance
- Health insurance is mandatory in Germany. Nexora's payroll/benefits setup interacts
  with statutory health insurance for most employees.
- **Check the current official guidance for your location and circumstances** — the
  right insurance path can depend on salary level, employment history, and personal
  situation, and this checklist does not attempt to cover every case.

## Tax Registration
- You'll generally receive a tax ID (Steuer-ID) automatically after address
  registration.
- **Check the current official guidance for your location and circumstances** — Nexora
  HR is not able to provide personal tax advice.

## Mobile / SIM Card
- A local SIM (prepaid or contract) is easy to set up once you have a German address;
  some providers require it for a contract plan, prepaid options generally do not.

## Public Transport
- Munich and Berlin both have extensive public transport (U-Bahn, S-Bahn, tram, bus).
  Monthly or annual passes are common; some employees are eligible for a subsidized
  transport benefit — check with HR Generalist for current eligibility.

## Finding a General Practitioner (GP/Hausarzt)
- It's worth registering with a local GP even before you need one, as appointment
  availability can be limited, especially for new patients.

## Recycling & Waste Rules
- German households typically separate waste into multiple categories (paper, packaging,
  organic, residual, glass). Your building/landlord can usually explain the local system
  — it varies slightly by city and building.

## Internet Setup
- Home internet installation can take a few weeks from signup to activation in some
  buildings — it's worth arranging this as early as possible after securing housing.

## Common German Workplace Practices
- Punctuality for meetings is generally valued.
- Clear, direct written communication (e.g., in email/chat) is common and not intended
  as unfriendly — see communication_culture.md and germany_workplace_tone.md.
- A relatively firm separation between work hours and personal time is common; being
  contacted about non-urgent work matters outside hours is less common than in some
  other cultures.

## Emergency Contacts
- General emergency number (police/fire/ambulance) is widely known and posted in the
  office kitchen at both German locations. Nexora's Office Manager (Munich) maintains a
  local emergency contact sheet including the nearest hospital to the office.

## A Few Useful Everyday German Phrases
- "Guten Tag" — Good day (formal greeting)
- "Danke" / "Vielen Dank" — Thanks / Thank you very much
- "Entschuldigung" — Excuse me / Sorry
- "Sprechen Sie Englisch?" — Do you speak English?
- These are offered as friendly, practical basics — not a language course.

## Three Things People Commonly Forget (informal HR observation, not a formal statistic)
1. Registering the address promptly, since it blocks several later steps.
2. Setting up home internet early, given installation lead times.
3. Registering with a GP before actually needing one.
